A feature release: cafesf2 converts the music of Wii U games that play their sound through JAudio, the sound library in Nintendo EAD's JSystem. Twilight Princess HD and The Wind Waker HD are two of them. Sound archives convert as in 1.0.2.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later. No additional runtime DLLs are required. Drop one or more `.bfsar`, `.baa` or `.aaf` files onto `cafesf2.exe`. You can also use a terminal. `cafesf2 --help` lists the options. Output goes in a folder beside each input.

For macOS on Apple Silicon, follow the build instructions in the README. macOS builds include `Cafesf2.app`. Drop archives onto the app in Finder to convert them.

## New

- **JAudio games.** Some Wii U games have no sound archive. They play their sound through JAudio instead. Give cafesf2 the game's start-up archive, with the game's other sound files beside it as in the title's `content` folder. A game with JAudio2 has a `.baa` start-up archive, with `Seqs` and `Waves` folders beside it. Its sound effects convert with the music. A game with the first JAudio has an `.aaf` start-up archive, with `Seqs` and `Banks` folders and a name file (`.asn`) beside it. The game's code steers such a game's sound effects in one sequence. cafesf2 leaves that sequence out. The files go in `<archive name>_cafesf2` beside the archive.
- **Options for JAudio games.** `--loops`, `--seed` and `--only` work as for a sound archive. The game's random state starts at 0 unless `--seed` gives another. Ports start at 0, as in the game. `--voices N` drops the notes that JAudio would drop with N voices. The result only approximates the game. `--var` changes nothing in a JAudio game.

## Known limitations

As in 1.0.2, apart from the changes above. JAudio times its music for the GameCube's rate of about 32,028 Hz. The Wii U plays it at 32,000 Hz. There the music plays 0.089% slower and 1.5 cents lower than in the files. The fitted envelopes can't follow an oscillator's table that rises again or loops. Random instrument effects play at their base values. Tremolos, track filters, pitch envelopes and notes on the DSP's built-in oscillators are left out. A vibrato deeper than a semitone is kept to a semitone. In the first JAudio's sequences, a track that shares its parent's voices gets separate voices. The oscillators that a track sets and the tempos of child tracks are left out there. cafesf2 reports each of these for each sequence. See "JAudio games" and "Differences from the game" in the README for the full list.
